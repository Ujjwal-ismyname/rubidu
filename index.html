Why I'm building it

Every day I use a Mac, and every day Siri sits there feeling outdated. It is slow, it can't do much, and it never feels like it knows me. Meanwhile the models from Anthropic, OpenAI and the open-source community keep getting better.

So I wanted my own little Jarvis. Not another assistant that sends everything I say to a cloud, but something local, private and personal.

I dug into small open models that run on a laptop, voice AI and Mac automation, and decided to build it myself, with Claude, YouTube and far too many tabs. That became Rubidu.

Ujjwal, student, and the one person building Rubidu

What it does today

Talk to it, hands free

Say its name, or hold ⌘ ⌥ and talk. Tap twice and it keeps listening until you tap again.

It can speak in your voice

Read a short script aloud. Rubidu keeps the clearest 30 seconds and clones the voice on your Mac. It is never uploaded.

33 languages, Hinglish included

English, Hinglish and Hindi are measured. About thirty more are selectable.

It does real things

  • Open and quit apps
  • Timers and reminders
  • Volume and brightness
  • Play and pause music
  • Search the web
  • Read your screen, when asked
  • Notes and lists
  • Exact maths and units

Careful by default

Anything risky waits for your yes, and everything it does is listed for you to see. It cannot run shell commands or delete your files.

What happens when you speak

  1. It hears

    Your voice becomes text on your Mac. The audio is never uploaded.

  2. It understands

    It works out what you mean, with the last few things you said as context.

  3. It checks

    Before acting, it makes sure the action is allowed. Risky ones wait for your yes.

  4. It acts

    Through the Mac's own controls, the same ones you would use.

  5. It speaks

    In the built-in voice, or in yours.

Where it has run

  • Only on my MacBook Air (M2, 16 GB). 8 GB Macs and Intel Macs are untested.
  • It needs Apple silicon and macOS 26.3 or later.
  • No Windows version yet. That needs a Windows PC, which in this economy of RAM and SSD prices is a whole project of its own.

Rough edges

  • It is signed by me, not notarized by Apple, so the first open goes through Privacy & Security, then Open Anyway.
  • Short commands in a strong accent are the hardest to hear. It asks "Did you mean…?" when unsure.
  • Its windows are not fully readable by VoiceOver yet.
  • It cannot read your email or your notes aloud yet.

Questions

What is Rubidu?

Rubidu is a voice assistant for macOS that runs entirely on your Mac. You talk to it, it answers in a voice you choose, and it does things for you: opening apps, timers and reminders, music and volume, web searches, and reading your screen when you ask.

Does Rubidu send my voice or data to the cloud?

No. Hearing, thinking and speaking all happen on your Mac. There is no account and no telemetry. The only download is the AI models, once, when you press the button in Setup.

Does it work offline?

Yes. After the one-time model download in Setup, Rubidu works with no internet connection.

Can Rubidu speak in my own voice?

Yes. You read a short script aloud, and Rubidu learns your voice on your Mac. The recording is never uploaded, and you can delete it whenever you like.

Which Macs does it run on?

Macs with Apple silicon on macOS 26.3 or later. So far it has been tested on a MacBook Air with an M2 chip and 16 GB of memory.

Is there a Windows version?

Not yet. A Windows version is planned after the Mac launch.

When can I try it?

Rubidu is coming soon. Follow @heyrubidu on Instagram or YouTube to hear the moment it launches.
